I read [Daniel Greenheck's post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/danielgreenheck_i-think-its-time-to-hang-up-my-threejs-share-7508024146879094784-A2uC) today about possibly quitting Three.js development because of AI. It was a sad read.

<iframe src="https://www.linkedin.com/embed/feed/update/urn:li:share:7508024146879094784?collapsed=1" width="504" height="264" loading="lazy" title="Daniel Greenheck's LinkedIn post" class="embed"></iframe>

I'm nowhere near as talented or successful as him, but I think a lot of people feel the same way, me included.

The way I see it, there have always been two kinds of people who write code. Some have a product mindset. For them coding was never the important part, it was just how the product got built. They didn't care how things were structured, only that the thing worked. AI is a godsend for them. They can just build stuff now, and I think they're in a good place.

The head of product at my previous company was this kind of person. He had good product sense, he was also a UI designer, and he knew the domain. He was pretty non-technical, but when he started using these AI tools he quickly figured it out, and he was the happiest person I've ever seen use Claude. I'm not gonna lie, the code he produced was absolute slop. But he was shipping features and building things into the product by himself, and he felt empowered. So yeah, these people do exist.

Then there are the people who cared about the craft and getting better at it. For a lot of them I think AI has killed the motivation, because it took away the thing that drew them to programming in the first place. That's where the unfulfilled and bored feeling comes from, and I think more and more people who cared about the craft are going to end up there.

I don't think there's a fix for this. You can accept it and move on. Or you can try to change how you think about it and stop caring so much about the craft. That's easier said than done, though. How much you care about that stuff is pretty intrinsic, and I don't think you can just switch it off.

It's not a good place to be in. It's a sad time, really.
