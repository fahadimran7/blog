---
title: "Is everything AI slop now?"
date: 2026-10-17
description: "An engineer got called out for AI slop on an app he built by hand. Some thoughts on why that keeps happening."
tags: ["ai", "design"]
---

I recently came across a LinkedIn post by an engineer who's building a workout tracking app called [Bearly Fit](https://bearly.fit/). He's been working on it for years, since before AI was a thing. His post was about how people seem to think it's AI slop, that he just built it with AI. He had to clarify that all the animations and illustrations were done by him. There was real taste and work involved.

Which got me thinking: is everything AI slop now?

We've gotten to a point where the benchmark is always "make it human, it shouldn't look like AI slop". But what exactly is AI slop?

In the early days of these models, AI slop looked like purple gradients, shadows and glowing buttons, outlines everywhere, em dashes. Then came the [frontend design skill](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/tree/main/skills/frontend-design) from Claude, and other skills people wrote, and we slowly started to identify those things and cut them out to make the output more human.

The days of purple gradients are over. But the AI tells keep changing. Six months ago it was purple gradients. Now it's maybe those eyebrows Claude likes to add in all caps above every heading, that grotesque font it loves, and the warm editorial palette. We trained the models to stop doing the obvious sloppy stuff, and they just converged on a new common output.

Our goal is to make AI produce output like a human. But once it starts doing that, it becomes the new slop. A design that was fine six months ago is slop now because every agent is producing it. And this cycle keeps on repeating.

And it'll only get worse as agents get better at replicating UIs. Any UI a human made can now be copied with just a screenshot. As more and more people replicate these designs, it'll be even harder to tell what slop looks like.

So what will actual human output look like? If the goal is for AI output to look human, and all of it does, then real human work will trigger the same alarms, and we'll call it AI slop.

That's what happened to him. His genuine effort looked a lot like what AI produces nowadays, because we've asked these models, with all our skills and instructions, to produce output like humans. So what happens to the humans who made the original stuff? They get labeled AI slop, even though they never used it.

I'm not sure how this cycle ends, or how we actually identify craft moving forward. For now, I think it's really weird. It's an interesting time to be in. Sad and fascinating at the same time.
