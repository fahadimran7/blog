# Writing a post in my voice

Read this before turning my notes into a blog post. Most posts start with me talking into a microphone, so the input is usually a rambling voice transcript: repeated points, half sentences, "like", "basically", "you know", things said in the wrong order, words the dictation got wrong. Your job is to turn that into a post that reads like I sat down and typed it. Not like an AI wrote it, and not like a LinkedIn post.

The existing posts in `src/content/posts/` are the reference. If something here is unclear, read two or three of them and copy what they do.

## The main rule: it's my post, not yours

- **Use my words.** Build every sentence from what I actually said. Fix grammar, cut filler and repetition, reorder for flow, merge points I made twice. Don't swap my plain words for fancier ones.
- **Don't add opinions, facts or conclusions I didn't give you.** If a paragraph needs a connecting sentence to make sense, you can write one, but keep it short and plain and tell me which sentence it is.
- **Don't guess technical details about my own experience.** If I was vague about what happened in an interview or at work, stay vague or ask. A wrong detail is worse than a missing one.
- **Flag your guesses.** When the transcript is garbled and you're inferring a word or a name, say so in your reply (not in the post).
- **Keep my opinions sharp.** If I said something blunt, keep it blunt. Don't soften "it's pissing people off" into "it has sparked debate". Don't add balance I didn't ask for.
- **Lead with my take when I'm reacting to something.** If the post is about someone else's tweet, video or article, embed or link it near the top and spend the post on what I think. Don't summarize their content back to the reader.

## What my voice sounds like

Plain, chill, first person, like I'm talking to a friend who also writes code. A bit self-deprecating, honest about what I don't know, sometimes ranty.

- **Short paragraphs.** Usually 1–4 sentences. One-line paragraphs are fine for a punchline that's actually mine ("It's scary, man.").
- **Normal sentences.** Mostly simple subject-verb sentences, with "and", "but", "so" doing the joining. Starting sentences with "And", "But", "So" is fine; I do it a lot.
- **Casual words, including swearing when I used it.** "dogshit", "pissed off", "shit ton", "big-ass", "bullshit", "heck". Don't add swearing I didn't say, but don't clean it out either.
- **Internet-casual bits.** "ofc", "aka.", "duh", "(yes, me, ofc)", "I might be addicted." Keep them when they come from me.
- **Caps for emphasis, sparingly.** "I'm NOT saying…", "I DO believe…", "It BECOMES the new slop." One or two per post at most, and only where I'd stress it out loud.
- **Parenthetical asides.** Little jokes or admissions in brackets: "(I still can't spell it on the first try)", "(You shouldn't, though, as you can just build it yourself. That's the point of this article, duh.)".
- **Scare quotes for terms I'm skeptical of.** "10x devs", "feel" more human, "craft", "plan mode".
- **Honest hedging, not corporate hedging.** "I might be wrong about some of it", "I haven't looked into how people do it yet", "I don't know exactly when that stopped." Say what I don't know plainly, once. No "it's worth noting" or "arguably".
- **Admitting things.** "I'm probably very stupid for not availing that, and I admit it." "Call me a hypocrite if you want." Keep these; they're part of the tone.

Phrases that are mine and fine to reuse when they fit: "here's my two cents", "That's my take, anyway.", "Don't get me wrong", "Now whether you buy into all of this is on you.", "It got me thinking", "I recently came across", "Honestly,", "kind of", "pretty", "really", "a lot".

## How a post is usually shaped

There's no template, but most posts go roughly like this:

1. **Open with what happened.** What I saw, read, built or thought about, in one or two plain sentences. "I read Daniel Greenheck's post today about…", "I've been using T3 Code for the past few weeks." No hook, no question bait, no "Let's talk about X."
2. **The source, if there is one.** Embed or screenshot it right after the opening.
3. **My take**, in the order that makes the argument easiest to follow, even if I rambled it in a different order.
4. **End when the thought ends.** Usually a short, casual line that sounds like me trailing off: "That's my take, anyway.", "I think I'm going to do this a lot more. I might be addicted.", "It's a sad time, really." It can echo the title. It is never a moral, a lesson, a call to action, or a neat one-line summary of the post.

Length follows the ramble. Most posts are 150–600 words. A post can be two sentences and an image if that's all it is ("Hear, hear"). Don't pad.

**Headings:** most posts have none. Only use `##` sections if the post is long and actually has separate parts. Never add headings just to make it look organized.

**Lists:** only when I'm actually listing things ("There are two problems for me with LinkedIn: 1. … 2. …"), or quoting a bunch of arguments. Not as a default way to break up text.

**Technical posts:** explain things as simply as I explained them, in my order of reasoning. It's fine to say I'm still learning. Don't upgrade my explanation with jargon or extra correctness I didn't say. If I got something wrong, tell me in your reply rather than silently fixing it in the post.

## Never do these

These are the AI and LinkedIn tells I started this blog to get away from.

- **Em dashes (—).** Use a comma, a full stop, a colon or brackets instead. En dashes in number ranges are fine.
- **Hook openers:** "Here's the thing.", "Let's be honest.", "I bombed X. Here's what I learned.", "Nobody talks about…", a one-word line followed by a reveal.
- **Setup lines:** "What stuck with me was…", "The part that got me…", "Here's why that matters:", "The real question is…".
- **Slogan or summary endings:** "In the end, it's about X.", "The lesson? …", "And that's the point.", "Build things. Ship things." Also don't end on a question for the reader to answer.
- **Calls to action:** "Let me know what you think", "Curious to hear your thoughts", "Follow for more".
- **Dramatic fragment trios:** "No meetings. No tickets. Just code." and rule-of-three lists in general.
- **"It's not X, it's Y" contrasts**, unless I said exactly that.
- **AI words:** delve, crucial, pivotal, landscape, tapestry, journey, navigate, unlock, leverage, robust, seamless, game-changer, testament, quietly, curious, genuinely (unless I said it), "in today's world", "at the end of the day".
- **Bold labels at the start of paragraphs**, emoji, and hashtags.
- **Hedging pairs and filler:** "it's worth noting that", "interestingly", "to be fair", "in many ways".
- **Polish I wouldn't do.** If a sentence sounds like a press release or a TED talk, rewrite it plainer.

## Titles and descriptions

- **Title:** short and conversational, often a phrase from the post or something I'd say out loud. "It's scary, man", "You can just change it", "Here comes Murphy", "Is everything AI slop now?", "Do you actually read the code anymore?". Catchy is good, clickbait isn't. If I didn't give one, suggest two or three and pick the one that sounds most like me.
- **Description:** one plain line saying what the post is. "Raw thoughts after watching Kurzgesagt's video on where AI is heading." "Some thoughts after reading Daniel Greenheck's post about quitting Three.js." No selling.

## Media

When I mention something I saw, include it. Copy the exact markup from the post that already does it:

- **Screenshot or image** with a credit line underneath: see `2026-09-16-n-plus-1.md`. Images go in `src/content/posts/images/`.
- **LinkedIn post embed:** `2026-10-13-sad-time-for-the-craft.md`.
- **YouTube embed** (lazy, click to play): `2026-10-08-its-scary-man.md`.
- **Local video:** `2026-10-21-just-change-it.md` (files in `public/posts/<slug>/`).
- **GIF:** `2026-09-15-why-this-blog-exists.md`.

Link people's names to their profile and claims to a source, like the existing posts do. If I mention a link I didn't give you, ask me for it or leave a clear placeholder and tell me.

## Before you hand it back

- Read the post next to one or two old ones. Does it sound like the same person?
- Search for `—`, then for the banned words above.
- Check the ending isn't a summary or slogan.
- Make sure every claim and opinion came from me.
- In your reply, list:
  - any sentence you wrote that I didn't say,
  - any word or name you guessed from bad dictation,
  - anything you cut that I might want back.
- Then follow the "add blog post" steps in `CLAUDE.md` (front matter, tags, `npm run build`). Don't commit or push unless I ask.
